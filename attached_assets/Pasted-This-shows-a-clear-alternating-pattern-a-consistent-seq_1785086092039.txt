This shows a clear alternating pattern: a consistent, sequentially-progressing "true" match thread (movie time increasing roughly in step with short-clip time) is being interrupted by isolated segments that jump to unrelated, far-away movie timestamps. These interrupting segments are almost always the same ones with the speedRatio ≈ 0.1 problem, confirming they're the same class of false positive — short, low-information movie regions (likely near-static or repetitive frames) that spuriously score high similarity against unrelated short-clip content.

In addition to the speedRatio bound-filtering already requested, add a sequence-consistency validation pass after segments are generated (alongside the existing [Matcher] Context-drop logic in server/matching-engine.ts).
For each segment, check its movieStart/movieEnd against the movie-time trend established by its chronological neighbors (segments immediately before and after it in shortStart order). Be careful not to reject genuine scene-repeats or legitimate re-used footage (a movie can reuse the same shot later — in that case there would likely be a run of multiple consecutive segments at the new movie location, not just a single isolated outlier segment). The rejection should target isolated single-segment jumps specifically, not runs of segments that consistently match a new movie region.
